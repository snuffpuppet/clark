# Runbook S4: Processes

Version 0.2, 23 September 2026.

## Purpose

Build each business process the session describes as a flow: who starts it, on what trigger, which people and systems act in turn, and the outcome it ends in. Then present each flow for review as a whole, write it into the record, and hand the gaps back as a walkthrough agenda for the next session.

This stage exists because stakeholders do not tell a process in one place. The audit behind it (`ingester/process-audit-T001-T002.md`) found:

- one sale told five times across nine episodes;
- corrections that insert a step spoken last into first place;
- variants given as a contrast in one breath;
- steps performed by teams who were not in the room.

A process read exchange by exchange becomes a list of statements about it. S4 reads the whole session with one question in hand, "what happens next, and who does it", and it runs after S3 so that it reads the written register with real ids.

## Inputs

- `sessions/Tnnn/Tnnn.utterances.tsv`, the whole of it.
- The signed session sheet, for roles, segments and standing.
- `ingester/extraction-rules.md`, R1, R3, R4, R7, R10, R13, R17 and R20 to R25 in particular.
- `ingester/extraction-solution-design.md` section 4.1, the definition of a process, a step and a process fact.
- The engagement as S3 left it:
  - every file under `processes/`;
  - every file under `systems/`, because flow fragments phrased with a system as the subject were often recorded as system facts;
  - `stakeholders/`;
  - `open-items/`;
  - `index/walkthrough-agenda.md`.
- The signed dossier `sessions/Tnnn/Tnnn.dossier.md`, for its episode boundaries and titles. Its episode ids are what a step's `- Episode:` names.

## Command

```
ingester/bin/stage <engagement> Tnnn status
```

prints `needs-s4` once S3 has written the transcript. The reading is the skill (`/ingest-transcript Tnnn` from inside the engagement folder); a person doing it by hand follows the same procedure. The write is `ingester/bin/stage <engagement> Tnnn S4` once the processes file is signed.

## Procedure

### 1. Read

Read, in this order:

1. `extraction-rules.md` in full, and design 4.1.
2. Every process file and every system file.
3. The open items that `clarify` a process claim, and `index/walkthrough-agenda.md`. These are the questions a previous session left for this one; each one this session answers is a mutation (R17).
4. The session sheet.
5. The dossier's episode headings.
6. The whole utterance table, end to end.

### 2. Catalogue

List the processes the session touches, before collecting any detail. For each one, write down:

- who starts it;
- its trigger;
- its outcome (what exists when it is done, and for whom).

Match each against the existing processes by trigger and outcome (R21), never by the interviewer's heading.

- A match is the existing PRC id.
- A capability, a segment, a tool or a condition is not a process. Commission capture is a run of steps inside the sale, multi-site is a condition on quoting, and Residential against SMB is a `when`.
- Write the catalogue at the top of `sessions/Tnnn/Tnnn.process-evidence.md`, one line per process: the PRC id or `new`, trigger, outcome, upstream and downstream processes.
- A handoff to a team in another function that works on to an outcome of its own is a boundary between two processes (R21): the business quote ends when sales emails the signed quote to service delivery, and delivery is a process of its own. Where the session does not show who owns the second half or what it ends in, keep one process and raise a Question on the boundary.
- Revise the catalogue whenever a later step shows two processes are one, or one is two. Merging two existing processes is a mutation that Withdraws one with `replaced-by` naming the other. Splitting one is a new process plus mutations that Withdraw the moved claims with `replaced-by`.

### 3. Harvest

For each process, collect every passage in the whole transcript that tells part of it, whatever the episode. Search by:

- the nouns of its actors, systems and objects, allowing for transcription noise (Quosal, Quozal, Causal; Wide Sales, White Sales, wide cells);
- sequence words ("then", "once they", "the following morning", "after that");
- corrections ("well, actually", "no, on the website").

Include:

- retellings at other depths;
- the passage an interviewer's question drew out;
- passages already recorded as system facts or as claims in the wrong process. Name those claim ids beside them.

Write each process's passages into `Tnnn.process-evidence.md` as a table: utterance range, speaker, episode, short quote, existing claim id if any. This file is a working file, like the exchanges file. It is kept, and the scorer and a reviewer can read it, but it is not reviewed.

### 4. Decompose

Break every passage into single actions (R20). Each action has:

- an actor;
- an action and its object;
- a system;
- a handoff (the next actor or process);
- a condition.

A change of actor is a new action. Build each citation with `ingester/bin/quote` and keep the continuation clause that carries the next action.

**Resolve the actor.** Work out "we", "they", "someone in my team" and a passive verb from the passage, the episode and the session sheet (R23). When the actor cannot be resolved, write `unresolved`. A system that does its turn is the actor.

**Separate facts from actions.** Anything that is not an action is a process fact: a volume, a timing, an SLA, a routing rule or a pain point (R22). Note the step it qualifies.

**Set confidence by whose work it is (R24).** It is Stated when the speaker or their own team performs the step. It is Second-hand when they describe another team. Hedges and challenges apply as always (R3, R4). An architect's or consultant's paraphrase that no one who does the work confirmed is a Question (R13).

### 5. Assemble

Order each process's actions into a flow from trigger to outcome.

- **Order.** Use sequence words and dependencies; never order by timestamp. A correction that says a step comes first puts it first.
- **Merge retellings.** Two actions with the same actor and the same action are one step, with the evidence of both.
- **Record variants.** A variant (segment, channel, tool, condition) is recorded with `when` on the steps that differ, and the steps both variants share are written once. Where variants rejoin, the joining step `follows` the last step of each.
- **Close the flow.** The last step either reaches the outcome or `hands-to` the downstream process.
- **Contradictions.** Two tellings that disagree are both recorded Contested, with a Question (R25).

### 6. Walk the flow

Walk each flow from trigger to outcome and list what is missing (R25). Each of these is a Question aimed at the role that performs the step:

- **No handoff.** Two steps with no stated handoff between them, where the actor or the system changes with nothing to carry the work across.
- **No end.** No step reaches the outcome.
- **Unresolved actor.** A step whose performed-by is `unresolved`.
- **Second-hand step.** A step told only by someone who does not do it.
- **Unanswered prompt.** An item on an interviewer's checklist that nobody answered.
- **Unconfirmed paraphrase.** A flow told only by an architect or consultant.

A gap that needs someone to act before the next session is an OI:
- its Owner is the stakeholder in that role if the register holds one, otherwise whoever raised it;
- its Links carry `clarifies PRC-nnnn.sN` for the claim it concerns (`item nn` in the same file).

Everything else is a Question.

### 7. Existing claims

Compare the assembled flow with every existing claim that the harvest named. For each, propose one of:

| Existing claim | Mutation |
|---|---|
| Confirmed as a step | Evidence added, and `follows`, `when` or `hands-to` set |
| Compound, a fact, or in the wrong process | Status Withdrawn, with `- Replaced by:` naming the items that replace it |
| A system fact that is really a step | Withdrawn and replaced by the step, or kept when it is also stated as a capability, with the step naming the system |

The Gist of every mutation states each change as `Field: old to new`. Where an existing process's Title, Trigger, Performed by or Systems no longer describes the flow, a PRC mutation corrects them, and an Outcome is always proposed.

### 8. Write the processes file

Write `sessions/Tnnn/Tnnn.processes.md` from `ingester/templates/processes.md`, in the F13 shape.

**Sections.** One section per process in the catalogue, `## Tnnn-P01 <title>`. Under the heading:
- `- Process:` is `new` or the PRC id;
- `- Flow verdict:` is blank;
- `- Bulk accept:` is blank.

**Flow table.** Then a `### Flow` table: the assembled flow, one row per step in flow order, with the item number of each. Facts and Questions are not rows.

**Items.** Then the items, numbered from 01 through the whole file, in this order:
1. the PRC item (new, or a mutation);
2. the steps in flow order;
3. the process facts;
4. the Questions and OIs;
5. the mutations of existing claims.

**Linking.** A step's `- Follows:` names earlier items or existing steps, never a later item. An item in one section may refer to an item in another (a `hands-to` a new process).

**Episode and grade.**
- Every step's `- Episode:` is the episode of its first citation.
- Grade every item under the grading conditions. Second-hand steps are graded Needs a human: standing unclear.

**Header.** Set Transcript, Session date, Ingester version, Rules version, New items and Mutations.

### 9. Walkthrough agenda

Write `sessions/Tnnn/Tnnn.walkthrough-agenda.md` from `ingester/templates/walkthrough-agenda.md`.

**Per process:**
- its flow so far, as step titles in order;
- then, grouped under the role who should answer:
  - every gap from step 6;
  - every contradiction;
  - every Second-hand step, to hear it from the people who do it.

Each line names its item number, so that once written it resolves to an id.

**Asking for a walkthrough.** Where a process was told only in fragments, the agenda asks for a walkthrough of it by the role that performs it, from trigger to outcome. The audit found that a walkthrough by someone who does the work is the one kind of prompt that reliably produces a flow.

### 10. Self-checks

Run each, fix what can be fixed, rerun, and record what remains at the end of the file under `## Closing`.

1. `ingester/bin/check-citations <engagement> Tnnn sessions/Tnnn/Tnnn.processes.md` prints OK.
2. `ingester/bin/check-integrity <engagement> --proposed sessions/Tnnn/Tnnn.processes.md` passes. In particular:
   - I21: `follows` names an earlier step in the same process;
   - I22: fact kinds;
   - I23: every Current process has an Outcome;
   - I24: every Withdrawn claim names what replaced it.
3. Every Flow table row names an item that is a PRC.step in that section, and every PRC.step in the section is a row.
4. No step's Title joins two actions with "and" where two actors act, and none carries a volume, a timing or a reason. A reason is a fact, or goes in the Gist.
5. Every step whose performed-by is `unresolved`, and every Second-hand step, has a line on the agenda.

### 11. Accept the Confident items and present

**Accept.** For every process whose items are all Confident, write the reviewer's name on `- Bulk accept:`. It counts only once the reviewer gives the Flow verdict. Leave every Flow verdict blank: the flow is the reviewer's to judge.

**Log.** Write a run log with `ingester/bin/stage <engagement> Tnnn log S4 "<outcome>"`, and append to `LOG.md`:

```
- <today>: Tnnn S4 read; <n> processes (<new> new, <changed> changed), <steps> steps, <facts> facts, <questions> questions; awaiting review.
```

**Present** only what needs the reviewer's ruling, as the dossier gate does (runbook S1 step 8):

1. **Rulings.** Each flow or item that is genuinely uncertain, one short block each: what it says, the one quote that carries it, why it is uncertain, and a recommended verdict. That is a flow whose order, boundary or outcome the evidence does not settle, and an item graded Needs a human whose reason is not complete work. Second-hand, hedged and contested steps and facts, and Questions, are complete work: they are recorded as such and put on the walkthrough agenda, and are not listed.
2. **Or, when there are none**, the one line: no flows or items need your ruling; reply proceed to write the processes.
3. **The ask.** One line: reply proceed to take every recommendation and Accept every other Flow verdict and item, or rule on any by number.

The Flow tables, withdrawn claims and the agenda stay in the processes file and are not read aloud. A reply of proceed, accept all, go or yes writes the recommended verdict on each listed flow or item, Accept on every other Flow verdict and item, and Completed by and Completed on. Then stop.

## Outputs

- `sessions/Tnnn/Tnnn.process-evidence.md`: working file.
- `sessions/Tnnn/Tnnn.processes.md`: the review file.
- `sessions/Tnnn/Tnnn.walkthrough-agenda.md`.
- After the write:
  - new and changed process files;
  - changed system facts;
  - new open items;
  - session log rows and `- S4 written on:`;
  - `index/processes.md` in flow order;
  - `index/walkthrough-agenda.md`.

## After the write

Rerun `ingester/bin/stage <engagement> Tnnn summary`. It measures S4 on its own window and adds it to `evaluation/Tnnn-summary.md` and `evaluation/ingestions.md` (runbook summary); present the S4 figures in one short block.

## Automatic checks

At the write, in order, and the stage refuses at the first failure:

1. S3 has written the transcript, and S4 has not.
2. The processes file carries Completed by and Completed on.
3. Every Flow verdict and every item verdict is filled, under F13's bulk rule.
4. `check-citations` passes on every accepted item.
5. `check-integrity --proposed` passes on the accepted set merged with the engagement.

A failure after the checks restores every item folder and the session log.

## What the reviewer does

Read each Flow table as the people who do the work would, and ask whether that is what happens, in that order, from that trigger to that outcome. Then:

- **Flow verdict.** Write one on each process: `Accept`, `Edit` (after editing items: reorder by changing `- Follows:`, split or merge steps, move a step to another process) or `Reject` (nothing in the section is written; say why under Closing).
- **Needs a human items.** Only the rulings are presented; second-hand, hedged and contested items and Questions are complete work and are accepted with the flow. The Confident items of a bulk-accepted process are accepted with the flow.
- **Wrong process boundaries.** Where two processes are one or one is two, say so under Closing. The skill reworks the file.
- **Tell the skill.** Verdicts given in the terminal are enough (skill, Review happens in the terminal).

## What approval means

Each accepted flow reads as the work is done: a trigger, steps in order with an actor on each, and an outcome. Every gap in it is on the agenda.

## On rejection

Write `Reject` on the Completed by line and notes under Closing. The skill writes `Tnnn.processes.v2.md` and keeps the rejected file.

## Rebuilding a transcript written before S4

A transcript written by ingester 0.9.1 or earlier has process items from S1 and no processes file. S4 runs on it unchanged: `stage` reports `needs-s4`, and the existing claims from that transcript are the input to step 7. Rebuild in transcript order (T001 before T002), because R17 reads the flow the earlier rebuild wrote.
