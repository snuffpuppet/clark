# Runbook S0: Prepare

Version 0.4, 14 September 2026.

## Purpose

Turn the transcript file as received into the utterance table that every citation points at, and produce the session sheet that fixes who spoke, in what role, with what standing and decision authority. Every downstream rule depends on the roles confirmed here (design 4.6, R18).

## Inputs

- The VTT file, either a path outside the repository (first invocation) or already registered in `engagements/<name>/transcripts/` and sitting in `transcripts/unprocessed/`.
- Every file under `engagements/<name>/stakeholders/`.
- Optionally the meeting subject.

## Command

First invocation for a transcript:

```
ingester/bin/register-transcript <engagement> <path-to-vtt> ["meeting subject"]
ingester/bin/stage <engagement> Tnnn S0
```

Or, as the skill from inside the engagement folder: `/ingest-transcript <path-to-vtt> ["meeting subject"]`. The skill runs both commands and then follows the procedure below.

## Procedure

`stage ... S0` writes `sessions/Tnnn/Tnnn.utterances.tsv` and `sessions/Tnnn/Tnnn.session.md` with every speaker matched against `stakeholders/*.md` by name or variant. Speakers it could not match appear in Attendees with STK `new` and again in New stakeholders with empty cells. The reader (the skill, or a person doing it by hand) then:

1. Reads the whole utterance table.
2. For every `new` speaker, fills the New stakeholders row: Organisation (the real company name, never a positional label: the client's own name for internal people, the vendor's name for the vendor, and every other party by name), Role (Internal SME, Internal architect, Internal other, Vendor, Consultant), Segment (Residential, BE&G or Wholesale when the person speaks for one customer segment; blank when they speak for the whole business, as a Finance SME does; blank for vendors and consultants), Department (where they sit, such as Product, Operations, Finance, Architecture; blank when the transcript gives no clue), Standing (the systems, processes or domains they show they own or operate, as text), Decides (the subjects on which they may accept a decision; blank for architects and vendors), and Passage: one F2 citation for the utterance that best shows the role. The role is read from what they say and how others address them, never from the name alone. A `(Vendor)` suffix on the speaker tag is evidence, not proof.
3. Proposes Mentioned rows for people, roles or bodies named as owners or deciders who did not speak: "someone from the business", "ask NETCO", a named colleague, an approving group. Role Mentioned, or Forum for an approving body on our side, with Decides naming what that body approves. One citation each.
4. Proposes the Session date from the invitation, the reviewer's instruction, or the transcript, in `D Month YYYY` form, and the Domain. Fills the Meeting subject if one was given. Writes anything the reviewer should know, including the purpose inferred for the session, under Notes for the reviewer.
5. Leaves every Verdict cell blank, and Approver and Approved on blank.
6. Runs `ingester/bin/check-citations <engagement> Tnnn sessions/Tnnn/Tnnn.session.md`, fixes any FAIL and reruns until OK.
7. Appends to `LOG.md`: `- <today>: Tnnn S0 proposals written for <n> speakers; awaiting the session sheet.`
8. Presents the proposals for review in the terminal, so the reviewer never has to open the sheet to check them: a table with one row per proposed stakeholder and the columns Name, Org, Role, Segment, Department, Utterances (blank for people who did not speak) and Verdict (left blank); then a Decision authority list giving the full Decides text for every row that has one, because that is the field R19 reads at S1 and it is too long for the table; then one line naming any row whose evidence is thin; then the sheet path, the count matched and the count new. It says that verdicts can be given in the terminal and will be written into the sheet with the Approver and Approved on date on the reviewer's word, or that the sheet may be edited by hand instead. Then it stops.

## Outputs

- `sessions/Tnnn/Tnnn.utterances.tsv`: one row per cue, sorted by utterance then fragment (format F1).
- `sessions/Tnnn/Tnnn.session.md`: header, Attendees table with each speaker matched to a stakeholder file or marked `new`, New stakeholders table with the proposed role, segment, department, standing and Decides for each new speaker and for people named but absent, one citation each.
- A run log under `logs/`.

## Automatic checks

- The file's SHA-256 matches the transcript register. A mismatch blocks.
- `stage` refuses S0 when the sheet already exists.
- `ingester/bin/check-citations` passes on the sheet before it is presented.

## What the reviewer does

Confirm the Session date and Domain. Every proposal is presented as a table in the terminal, so this can all be done in conversation: say which rows are Correct or Accept and which need a change, and the skill writes the verdicts, the Approver and the Approved on date into the sheet. Editing the sheet by hand does the same job. For each attendee, Verdict is Correct or Edit, confirming the speaker is who the STK column says; a correction to an existing stakeholder's role or standing goes through an STK.edit item in the dossier under R18, not through this table. For each proposed stakeholder, write Accept, Edit or Reject. Segment is Residential, BE&G or Wholesale when the person speaks for one customer segment and blank when they speak for the whole business; Department is where they sit. Decides is the field that lets an SME's acceptance make a decision Accepted (R19), so it deserves care: normally the SME's area of expertise; blank for architects and vendors; for a Forum row, what that body approves.

## What approval means

The roles, standing and decision authority are right for everyone who spoke, and the stakeholder register is complete for this session. A blank verdict is pending and blocks S1.

## On rejection

There is nothing to reject: the sheet is the register's proposal. Edit it until it is right and sign. To start S0 again, delete the session sheet by hand and rerun; the utterance table is regenerated from the unchanged transcript.
